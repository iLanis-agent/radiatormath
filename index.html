Radiator math

A radiator bought by eye heats the hallway, not the room. Radiator math turns the room's dimensions into the watts it asks for on a labeled scale, corrects the catalog's rating for the water temperature your system actually runs, and turns the needed watts into a section count. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Room plan - length, width, height and a labeled watts-per-cubic-meter factor become the watts and BTU the room asks for.
  • Delta plan - flow, return and room temperatures become the real mean-water difference, and the labeled correction table turns the catalog rating into honest output.
  • Section plan - the needed watts against the output per section become a section count and the headroom it leaves.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 30 to 50 W per cubic meter by insulation, a delta-T correction table (50 C: full, 40 C: three quarters, 30 C: about half, 20 C: under a third, below: a trickle) - all illustrative. Real heat loss moves with windows, walls and weather. This is arithmetic, not a design: a heating engineer sizes the real system, and the manufacturer rates the real hardware.
